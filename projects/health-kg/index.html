Health Knowledge Graph

From input factors to organ-level scores — and the reasons behind every point. An interactive prototype showing how structured measurements can flow through a transparent knowledge graph to health states, organs and illustrative recommendations.

03

Explainable report

Every deduction can be traced from an input factor, through a health state, to an organ. Weights are teaching values chosen for this demo — not validated clinical coefficients.

04

Illustrative recommendation categories

Candidate actions are retrieved from a small, hand-written demonstration list and ranked by how strongly the active health states point to them. They are generic wellness themes, not prescriptions, and never replace professional advice.

05

How the demo is built

A · Factors → severity

Each input is mapped to a 0–1 severity with a simple piecewise-linear band curve. The curves are illustrative teaching values, not clinical cut-offs.

B · States → organs

Severities combine into ten health-state loads. Each state removes up to a fixed number of points from the organs it influences. Two linear layers keep every point traceable.

C · Recommendations

Active states retrieve candidate actions. In a real system every edge would need provenance, evidence grading, uncertainty, contraindication checks and clinical governance.

Limitations. Organ “scores” are not measurements of organ function. Real organ assessment needs imaging, specialised tests, history and clinical judgment. The model ignores age, sex, medications, genetics, conditions, and interactions beyond the simple additive structure above.
